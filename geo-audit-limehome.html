AI Search Visibility Audit: Limehome, October 2026

20 customer questions · 4 AI engines · 80 answers · 28 October 2026

LimehomeMethodMarketing case studies →
What was tested
ChatGPT (logged out) · Claude (new free account, memory paused) · Perplexity (logged out) · Google AI Overview (personalisation off)

Company: Limehome, a serviced-apartment operator based in Munich with 107 locations in Europe (own website, 28.10.2026). Not a client. This is a public test of public pages.

Question: when a traveller asks an AI assistant where to stay, does the answer name Limehome? If not, who does it name, and what do their pages have that Limehome's pages do not?

How: I wrote 20 questions a real guest would ask, without any brand name in them (for example "serviced apartment in Munich for one month with self check-in"). I asked each question once in each of four AI engines, as a guest with no history, and recorded every brand the answer named and every website it cited. Then I opened the pages of the three most-named operators and three Limehome city pages and compared them.

The result

Limehome was named in 4 of the 80 answers, each time inside a list, never as the recommendation for a city. The four are shown below with proof.

By engine: ChatGPT 1 of 20, Google AI Overview 1 of 20, Claude 2 of 20, Perplexity 0 of 20.

17 of the 20 questions name a city. Limehome operates in 16 of them (all except Zurich; for the Italy question it has Florence but not Bologna). In those 16 cities, 64 answers, it was named 3 times: Munich twice, Vienna once. Madrid, its largest city with 15 properties: 0 of 4 answers. Berlin, 12 properties, two questions: 0 of 8. The fourth mention came on a Germany-wide question with no city.

The four mentions, with proof

Every "yes" in the sheet has a screenshot taken at the moment of the answer, with the Limehome line highlighted. The ChatGPT and Claude answers can also be opened by anyone via their share links. The shared ChatGPT page shows the answer but not the prompt, so the prompt is given in the caption; the shared Claude pages show both.

The other 76 answers are recorded in the sheet with the brands named and the sources shown. All 20 ChatGPT answers and all 20 Claude answers have public share links there. Perplexity guest threads cannot be shared and were not screenshotted; this is a limit of the test, stated below.

Who gets named instead

Figure 1. Count A: the number of answers, out of 80, in which each brand was named, counted once per answer. Adina is a chain present in almost every city asked, so part of its count is geography. The sheet lists every answer and every name.

Three types of site win: operators with a written page per city (Adina, Brera, ipartment, STAYERY, Numa), marketplaces (Wunderflats, Flatio, Blueground, Booking.com), and third-party lists and review sites (Tripadvisor, Apartmentservice, travel blogs).

Where the engines get their information

Figure 2. Count B: the number of answers, out of 60, in which each website was shown as a source. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overview only (Claude shows sources as partial labels, see limits). A different count from Figure 1 with a different base; read side by side, never ranked against each other.

Why this matters. Figure 1 says how often a brand is named. Figure 2 says where the engine got the words it used. The gap between the two is the finding. Brera is named in 12 of 80 answers, and brera.de is shown as a source in 10 of 60: the engines read Brera's own pages and repeat what they say. Limehome is named in 4 of 80, and limehome.com is a source in 1 of 60; the other three mentions came through Tripadvisor, routard and bookabach. When an engine learns about you from a third-party site, you do not control the description, the price, the facts, or whether you appear at all. When it learns from your own page, you do. The page comparison below shows why Limehome's own site is almost never the source.

The cause in one sentence

AI engines quote text. The operators that get named have a written page per city that answers the guest's question; Limehome's city pages are search results with no text, so there is nothing to quote, and the few mentions it gets come through booking sites instead.

Three fixes

1. A written city page for Madrid, Berlin and Munich first, then the rest.

One first sentence that answers the guest's question, headings that use the guest's words (self check-in, kitchen, monthly stay, washing machine, pets), an FAQ block, guest reviews. Model: ipartment's Cologne page. Applies to limehome.com/listings/properties?city=82 (Madrid), ?city=32 (Berlin), ?city=45 (Munich).

2. Be on the lists the engines read.

Three of Limehome's four mentions came via Tripadvisor, routard and bookabach, none via Apartmentservice or business-travel roundups, the sources behind the Brera, ipartment and STAYERY mentions in the business-traveller question. A listing and a reviews profile on those sites costs little and is read by every engine.

3. One page for stays of weeks to months.

13 of the 20 questions were about stays of two weeks or longer (52 answers). Limehome was named in 1 of them, at the end of a list. The names that came back were Brera, Wunderflats, Blueground, Adina, Flatio and, for company bookings, ProjektStay and MA-Flats. A page that says weekly cleaning, kitchen, washing machine, company invoicing and monthly rates would give the engines a reason to name Limehome for these questions.

Limits of this test

One run per question per engine, one day. Answers vary between runs: Google reworded the Munich answer on a rerun, Claude's first pass differed on five questions. ChatGPT and Claude answers can be checked by anyone via the share links in the sheet. Perplexity guest threads cannot be shared, so only the sheet rows exist for them. Google links re-run the search rather than freeze the answer, and the Google run came from a signed-in browser located in Munich, not a clean guest session.

Claude is included in the brand count (Count A, 80 answers). It is excluded from the count of source websites (Count B, 60 answers) because its interface shows sources as short labels with hidden extra sources, so a domain count from it would be incomplete. Claude's first pass was run with memory switched on and was discarded after memory carried Limehome from one question into the next; all 20 questions were rerun with memory paused, and only the rerun is in the sheet. Source domains for Perplexity and Google come from the visible citation badges; hidden sources are not counted.

Nothing on this page claims an effect on traffic or bookings. It shows what the engines said on one day and what the pages contain.
